- **Breaking: removing a user's `Administrator` grant now ends that user's JIT elevation window, the unassign audit detail changes shape, and the unassign has a new `503` cause.** Before, `DELETE /api/v1/rbac/roles/Administrator/assignments/{principal_id}` and the MCP tool `unassign_rbac_role` left an already-open elevation standing until its window lapsed, so a user whose administrator authority had just been withdrawn kept administrator access (including destructive dispatch that otherwise needs approval) for the rest of that window. The same database transaction now locks the user's durable session rows and clears their elevation, so the removal and the clear commit together or not at all; if the clear fails the removal is rolled back and the route answers `503` (MCP: an internal error with a retry hint), although after a lost commit acknowledgement the removal may have committed, so read the grant list before assuming otherwise. The clear runs only when a grant row was actually removed: unassigning `Administrator` for a user who holds no such grant (the usual just-in-time user, who elevates through eligibility alone) still answers success with `removed=false` and ends nothing, so to end that user's access revoke their elevation eligibility (`POST /api/v1/users/{name}/elevation-eligibility` with `{"eligible": false}`, a REST-only step: it refuses MCP-tier and service-scoped tokens and no MCP tool exists for it yet). The replica that handled the call drops its cached sessions of the user after the commit, and other replicas converge on their next session-generation poll (about 1 s, up to about 30 s while that read is failing or slow; neither bound holds after a database restore or a failover to a lagging standby, so restart every replica then). Response bodies are unchanged. When the clear changed N > 0 session rows that held an elevation window (an expired window not yet reaped is counted), the `rbac.role.unassigned` audit detail ends with ` elevations_cleared=N`, joined to `removed=<bool>` by a single space, so match `removed=` as a token and not the whole text after it, and a `role.elevation.revoked` row (`reason=administrator_unassign`) is written for the user (the self-service `POST /api/v1/elevate/revoke` writes the same verb with `was_elevated=<bool>`, or `was_elevated=unknown` when its pre-clear session lookup was degraded); a retry after a lost commit acknowledgement writes neither. This does not change elevation eligibility and does not block `POST /api/v1/elevate`; a `POST /api/v1/elevate` already past its eligibility check can land just after the unassign commits, and group-held `Administrator` removal does not end an elevation. Upgrade note: the user `Administrator` unassign now waits at most 2000 ms for each lock (it waited up to the pool's 10 s default) and answers a retryable `503` when it cannot take its locks in time; look for the warning `rbac.role.unassigned store fault` and the counter `yuzu_server_rbac_lifecycle_store_failure_total{op="unassign"}`; requests already past their authorization check when the clear lands are not recalled. Separately, the elevate and MFA-stamp writes now drop the cached session instead of re-deriving it, closing a window in which a stalled writer could put a pre-revocation elevation back into the cache, at the cost of one extra durable read on the next request of that session.
