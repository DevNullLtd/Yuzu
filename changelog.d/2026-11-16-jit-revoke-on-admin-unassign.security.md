- **Removing a user's `Administrator` grant now ends that user's live JIT elevation window.** Before, `DELETE /api/v1/rbac/roles/Administrator/assignments/{principal_id}` and the MCP tool `unassign_rbac_role` left an already-open elevation standing until its window lapsed, so a user whose administrator authority had just been withdrawn kept administrator access (including destructive dispatch that otherwise needs approval) for the rest of that window. The same database transaction now locks the user's durable session rows and clears their elevation, so the removal and the clear commit together or not at all; if the clear fails nothing is removed and the route answers `503` (MCP: an internal error with a retry hint). The replica that handled the call drops its cached sessions of the user after the commit, and other replicas converge on their next session-generation poll (about 1 s, up to about 30 s while that read is failing). Response bodies are unchanged. When elevations were ended, the `rbac.role.unassigned` audit detail ends with ` elevations_cleared=N` and a `role.elevation.revoked` row (`reason=administrator_unassign`) is written for the user. This does not change elevation eligibility and does not block `POST /api/v1/elevate`: to end elevated access for good, revoke eligibility first (`POST /api/v1/users/{name}/elevation-eligibility` with `{"eligible": false}`), then remove the grant. Upgrade note: the user `Administrator` unassign now waits at most 2 s for each lock (it waited up to the pool's 10 s default) and answers a retryable `503` when it cannot take its locks in time; requests already past their authorization check when the clear lands are not recalled, and group-held `Administrator` removal does not end an elevation. Separately, the elevate and MFA-stamp writes now drop the cached session instead of re-deriving it, closing a window in which a stalled writer could put a pre-revocation elevation back into the cache.
