- **The fenced leader epoch now carries the Postgres timeline, narrowing the lossy-promotion hole (#6535).** The epoch is minted as `GREATEST((timeline << 32) + nextval(leader_epoch_seq), previous_epoch + 1)`. Under asynchronous replication, or a degraded non-strict quorum, a promoted standby could lack the latest election and re-mint the epoch a paused former leader still held, so the epoch fence admitted both; every promotion of a streaming standby starts a new timeline, so the new leader's epoch is now higher. **What you will see:** `yuzu_server_leader_epoch` and `leader_state.current_leader_epoch` read at least 4294967297 after the upgrade (the timeline is the value divided by 4294967296, rounded down), and an automatic migration caps the `leader_epoch_seq` sequence at 4294967295. **What can now go wrong:** the elector refuses to open if it cannot read the timeline (a DSN that reaches a standby, or a role that cannot run `pg_current_wal_lsn()` / `pg_walfile_name()`), which pauses the leader-only background passes until fixed (`cannot read the Postgres timeline` in the log, counted as `cause="connect"`). **Not covered** (listed in `docs/adr/2002-high-availability-architecture.md` section 3 and `docs/user-manual/ha-postgres.md`): two live primaries, a restore that loses `leader_state`, a lossy promotion while a restored row pins a higher timeline, same-timeline loss, a physical-snapshot restore without archive recovery, a rolling upgrade or a downgrade across this change (`docs/user-manual/upgrading.md` gives the one-line `issuer_epoch` reset). The safe-to-scale gate stays closed.
