- **Breaking - Echo trims an existing `echo.db` to a byte budget on the first maintenance pass after the upgrade.** An `echo.db` whose used pages exceed 245,366,784 bytes (the 250 MiB default less the 16 MiB log allowance) is trimmed about 15 minutes after the agent starts, or on any `echo.rollup`. The trim is permanent and needs no approval: it removes, oldest first, raw capture rows older than 24 hours that their first summary tier has already passed (hourly, or daily for `user` and `software`), then hourly rows older than 48 hours (5 days for `procperf`), even inside their retention window. Daily and monthly rows, the `usage` tables, paused or errored sources and sources with no summary tier are never removed. `retention_days` neither protects nor loses history. **Before upgrading an agent whose `echo.db` is near or above 250 MiB, export what you need with `echo.sql` by `ts` range, or send `echo.configure db_budget_bytes=<bytes>` within 15 minutes of its restart; an older agent cannot be pre-staged.** Lowering `db_budget_bytes` later deletes history within 15 minutes and raising it again does not restore it.
- **Echo records fewer raw capture rows by design.** A TCP socket first seen already closing is recorded once per hour per process and remote, outbound; for outbound connections the hourly `connect_count` for these sockets is distinct remotes per process per hour, and an inbound socket first seen closing is one row per client socket per hour, because the remote port is the client's ephemeral port. Module loads are stored once per hour per loader, module and folder, unloads are not stored, and risky loads are always stored. A registry change stores the new value up to 512 bytes and not the old value, the `bam\State` subtree is excluded, and a row's time is the key's last-write time.
- **Echo `db_size_bytes` counts used pages, and `echo.rollup` can delete inside the retention window.** `db_size_bytes` is now the used pages plus the write-ahead log, not every page in the file. `echo.rollup` now also deletes rows still inside their retention window when the database is over budget, and may run a one-off `VACUUM` that converts an older database to incremental auto-vacuum: collectors, `echo.status` and `echo.configure` wait for it, it needs twice the used size free on the volume, and otherwise `db_auto_vacuum` reads `deferred` and the conversion is retried within a day.
- **Echo fills `process_name` on TCP rows from the process source, and two classifications change.** `logon_type` on Linux and macOS follows `ut_host`, so a local terminal that read `remote` now reads `interactive` (older rows keep the old class). `echo_state` is rewritten only when a baseline changed.
