- **Guardian agent logs why a carried rule was rebuilt, not only which were skipped (#5719).** Every `full_sync` push that reaches the end of its rule loop now also logs one bounded line beside `Guardian: full_sync preserved ...`: `Guardian: full_sync rebuilt N rule(s) (rebuild attempted): first_attach=A content_changed=B key_busy=C faulted_key=D ...`, with a count for each of eleven reasons (`first_attach`, `content_changed`, `key_busy`, `faulted_key`, `dead_key`, `health_unknown`, `subscription_replaced`, `unknown_episode`, `baseline_empty`, `baseline_differs`, `other`). `first_attach` and `content_changed` are counts only; every other group follows its count with `ids=[...]`, all of them sharing one cap of 64 ids / 2048 bytes (`more=M` counts the ids left off), and the count includes rebuilds that may still fail afterwards. A health reason outranks a changed-content reason when both hold, and a kept rule on a faulted or dead key is reported with the key's cause rather than as `key_busy` or `first_attach`. The line is produced by the Spark attach path only, so while `prefer_spark` is off (the production default) it always reads `rebuilt 0`; it has been exercised on fake mechanisms, not on a flipped fleet. Alongside it, three in-process counters on the Spark runtime (`health_probe_failures`, `sweep_snapshot_failures`, `sweep_health_query_failures`) now count the key-health probe and sweep failures that were previously swallowed without a trace. They are not exported on the heartbeat or `/metrics`, and they stay zero while `prefer_spark` is off. The upgrading guide's Guardian full_sync log-lines entry and the Spark flip gate's precondition (b) describe it.
