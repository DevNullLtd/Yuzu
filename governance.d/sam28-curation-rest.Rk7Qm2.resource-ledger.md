## Decision lines

- **The two curation writes are one transaction each.** The plan allowed a product transaction then an alias transaction; `curate_alias` does both in one, because a product without its alias is an inert row and one transaction removes the window in which the dimension could be marked stale before the alias exists.
- **Dimension invalidation.** `curate_alias` and `replace_pack` take the blocking normalisation lock before writing and mark every `title_dimension` row `pack_version = ''` in the same transaction. The alias key cannot be reproduced in SQL, so no narrower predicate exists (as in `upsert_alias`). `reject_curation` takes no lock: a rejected row is preserved by the reconciler and is not a resolution input.
- **Pack retirement.** `replace_pack` sets every `pack:` alias `inactive` with `pack_version = ''` before the upserts; the upsert's `ON CONFLICT` re-activates the aliases the new pack still carries. Products the new pack drops stay as inert rows (nothing resolves to them through a pack alias).
- **No new body-cap row.** The pack body is at most 1 MiB (parser cap), below the 4 MiB default; a dedicated row would have changed the locked row and label counts for no tighter bound on the alias routes' small bodies.
